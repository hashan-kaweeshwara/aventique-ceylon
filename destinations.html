Sigiriya — The Lion Rock

Rising dramatically 200 meters above the surrounding plains, Sigiriya is one of South Asia's most spectacular archaeological sites. This UNESCO World Heritage Site was once the capital of King Kashyapa in the 5th century AD, who transformed the massive rock into an extraordinary palace and fortress complex.

Visitors can climb to the summit via ancient staircases, passing the famous cloud maiden frescoes — sensuous paintings that have survived over 1,500 years. The Mirror Wall, once polished to reflect the king's image, still bears ancient graffiti from visitors dating back to the 8th century. At the base, explore the remarkable water gardens, one of the oldest landscaped gardens in the world.

Key Attractions

  • Lion Rock Summit & Palace Ruins
  • Ancient Cloud Maiden Frescoes
  • Mirror Wall with Ancient Graffiti
  • Royal Water Gardens
  • Sigiriya Museum
  • Surrounding Forest Reserve

Kandy — Cultural Capital

Nestled among misty hills in the heart of Sri Lanka, Kandy is the island's spiritual and cultural capital. This UNESCO World Heritage city was the last royal capital of the Sinhalese kings and remains a living testament to Sri Lanka's rich Buddhist heritage and traditions.

The city's crown jewel is the Temple of the Tooth Relic (Sri Dalada Maligawa), which houses the sacred tooth relic of Lord Buddha — one of the most important Buddhist relics in the world. The temple is the focal point of the spectacular Esala Perahera festival, a grand procession of elephants, drummers, and dancers held annually in July or August.

Key Attractions

  • Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic
  • Royal Botanical Gardens, Peradeniya
  • Kandyan Cultural Dance Show
  • Gem Museum & Gallery
  • Tea Plantations & Factories
  • Kandy Lake & Promenade
